# Mentor Program

## Objective

Mentors help teams make decisions and unblock themselves without taking ownership of the project or influencing judging outcomes.

## Mentor preparation

Provide the event rules, challenge briefs, schedule, escalation contacts, venue map, sponsor limitations, accessibility guidance, code of conduct, and a list of supported technologies. Record each mentor’s expertise and availability.

## Interaction protocol

Mentors should begin with the team’s problem statement, current blocker, attempted solutions, and remaining time. Recommend the smallest next experiment. Explain trade-offs and leave the final decision with the team.

## Boundaries

Mentors must not write substantial project code, promise prizes, expose confidential information, pressure teams to use sponsor products, or provide different rule interpretations. Conflicts of interest must be disclosed.

## Operations

Use scheduled rounds plus an on-demand queue. Track recurring blockers so organizers can publish event-wide clarifications or workshops. Escalate safety, conduct, eligibility, infrastructure, and judging questions to the designated owner.

## Handoff record

A short mentor note should capture the team, blocker, advice, unresolved risk, and whether follow-up is required. Do not record unnecessary personal or sensitive information.
