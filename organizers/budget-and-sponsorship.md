# Budget and Sponsorship

## Budget model

Build the budget around participant safety and event delivery before prizes or branding. Separate committed funds, in-kind support, restricted sponsor funds, contingency, taxes, refunds, and post-event obligations.

Typical cost groups include venue or platform, accessibility, food, travel support, insurance, security, recording, hardware, cloud credits, communications, staff, prizes, payment fees, and contingency. Assign an owner, approval threshold, evidence requirement, and payment date to each line.

## Sponsorship principles

Sponsors may fund the event or provide technology without controlling general-track judging, participant data, safety decisions, or editorial outcomes. Publish what sponsors receive: recognition, challenge ownership, mentor access, booth space, aggregate reporting, or approved communications.

## Sponsor challenge design

A sponsor challenge must state the user outcome, required integration, eligibility, evidence, judging criteria, prize, data restrictions, and support route. Avoid requirements that force participants to expose secrets, purchase services, transfer excessive rights, or use unsafe preview features.

## Financial controls

Use written approvals, dual review for material payments, receipts, a vendor list, prize eligibility verification, and a reconciliation deadline. Do not announce a prize until funding, tax treatment, delivery method, and regional eligibility are confirmed.

## Post-event reporting

Report participant counts, eligible submissions, challenge usage, support demand, incidents, prize delivery, accessibility outcomes, and lessons learned. Provide aggregate results; do not share participant personal data beyond published consent and contractual necessity.
