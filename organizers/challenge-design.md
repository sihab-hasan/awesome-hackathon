# Challenge Design

## Outcome-first brief

Define the affected user, current workflow, desired outcome, constraints, available evidence, and judging standard. A challenge should permit multiple defensible approaches rather than prescribing one interface or architecture.

## Scope test

A viable hackathon challenge has a demonstrable vertical slice within the event duration, accessible data or simulators, documented APIs, realistic support capacity, and a clear distinction between prototype evidence and production claims.

## Required brief sections

- Problem and target users
- Why the problem matters
- Expected outcome, not predetermined solution
- Required and optional technologies
- Data sources and permitted use
- Security, privacy, accessibility, and safety constraints
- Eligibility and pre-existing-work rules
- Demonstration evidence
- Scoring criteria and tie-breaks
- Support contacts and service-status route

## Adversarial review

Before publishing, test whether the wording creates unfair regional, financial, language, disability, hardware, or account barriers. Review whether it encourages unsafe medical, financial, legal, surveillance, biometric, or high-impact decisions. Provide synthetic data or a non-production environment where appropriate.

## Change control

Freeze material challenge terms before the event. Corrections must be timestamped, visible to every team, and accompanied by an explanation of scoring or deadline impact.
