# Accessibility and Inclusion

## Planning baseline

Accessibility is an event requirement, not a participant exception. Publish a confidential accommodation route, response owner, venue or platform accessibility details, food information, sensory conditions, travel constraints, and available assistive support.

## Delivery controls

Use accessible documents, meaningful link text, readable contrast, captions, microphones, keyboard-operable platforms, accessible presentation templates, quiet space, flexible breaks, and alternatives to visual-only or audio-only instructions.

## Team and judging fairness

Do not penalize assistive technology, interpreters, prerecorded accessible material, remote participation, unfamiliar accents, or narrower scope chosen to protect reliability. Judge the demonstrated outcome against published criteria.

## Inclusion review

Review barriers related to disability, language, cost, location, identity documentation, age, caregiving, religion, hardware ownership, account payment methods, and prior technical experience. Provide loan equipment, team formation support, beginner routes, and clear behavioral expectations where feasible.

## Feedback

Collect accessible, optional feedback without requiring public disclosure. Track unresolved barriers and assign improvements before the next event.
