# Editorial Checklist

Use this checklist for every resource, guide, blueprint, project brief, and operational document.

## Accuracy

- The title names the actual subject.
- Claims are factual, bounded, and supported by primary or authoritative sources where available.
- Dates, versions, limits, prices, and policies are not presented as permanent.
- The text distinguishes demonstrated behavior from planned or inferred behavior.

## Utility

- The reader can identify when to use the item and when not to use it.
- The recommendation explains a concrete hackathon task rather than repeating marketing language.
- Trade-offs, prerequisites, failure modes, and safety considerations are visible.
- The smallest workable next action is clear.

## Curation

- The canonical resource is not already present under another name or URL.
- Affiliation is disclosed.
- Tracking parameters and affiliate links are absent.
- The source type, audience, stage, platform, risk level, and review date are correct.
- The entry remains useful without promotional ranking language.

## Writing

- Headings form a logical hierarchy.
- Sentences are direct and terminology is consistent.
- Links use descriptive text.
- Tables remain readable on narrow screens.
- Examples do not include secrets, personal data, unsafe commands, or unverifiable claims.

## Repository integrity

- Canonical data was edited instead of generated output.
- Internal links resolve.
- Generated files are synchronized.
- Structured validation and tests pass.
- Material changes are recorded in the changelog or release notes.
