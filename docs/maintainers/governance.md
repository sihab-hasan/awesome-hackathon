# Governance

## Mission

The repository provides a neutral, evidence-based field guide for hackathon participants, organizers, mentors, judges, and educators. Governance protects scope, editorial independence, contributor safety, schema stability, and release integrity.

## Roles

### Repository owner

Holds administrative responsibility, appoints maintainers, manages private security and conduct reports, and acts as final escalation when the documented process cannot resolve a decision.

### Maintainers

Review resources and documentation, operate releases, moderate contributions, enforce the curation policy, and maintain automation. Maintainers must disclose relevant affiliations and recuse themselves when impartial review is not possible.

### Category reviewers

Review a defined technical or operational area without requiring full repository administration. They verify source quality, metadata, duplicate coverage, and current relevance.

### Contributors

Propose focused changes, disclose affiliations, provide evidence, follow the code of conduct, and respond to review without pressuring maintainers for promotion or access.

## Decision model

- Routine documentation or metadata correction: one maintainer approval
- New resource or focused guide: one approval from a maintainer or relevant category reviewer
- New category, schema change, governance policy, or major restructuring: two maintainer approvals and a public proposal period
- Workflow, release, permission, or supply-chain change: independent maintainer review
- Security or conduct response: private review by the smallest authorized group

When consensus is unavailable, the repository owner records the decision and rationale. Decisions may be revisited when material new evidence appears.

## Conflicts of interest

Employment, investment, sponsorship, referral, ambassador, grant, or close project relationships must be disclosed. A conflicted reviewer may provide facts but cannot be the sole approver. Inclusion cannot be purchased or exchanged for promotion.

## Moderation

Maintainers may edit, hide, lock, or remove content and restrict participation when necessary to enforce repository policy or protect people. Private reports are handled with minimum necessary access and information.

## Maintainer appointment and removal

Consistent contributors may be invited after demonstrating sound review judgment, policy understanding, respectful collaboration, and reliable maintenance. Access follows least privilege. Maintainers may step down or be removed for inactivity, repeated policy violations, undisclosed conflicts, unsafe handling of reports, or loss of trust.

## Transparency

Public editorial decisions should be explainable through the curation policy, review comments, changelog, or release notes. Private security, conduct, safeguarding, and personal-data details remain restricted.
